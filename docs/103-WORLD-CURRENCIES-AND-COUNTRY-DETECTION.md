## 2. KNOWN is not COLLECTABLE, and that is the whole design

`CURRENCIES` (`src/lib/money/currencies.ts`) means **the platform can take
money in this**. Every entry carries a `gateway`, a donation floor and
ceiling and a pre-verification cap, and it is read by `checkDonationAmount`,
`checkRaiseCap`, `payment-service`, the PayHero client and the composite
foreign key on `(campaign_id, currency)`.

`WORLD_CURRENCY_EXPONENTS` (`src/lib/money/world-currencies.ts`) means
**this is a real currency and we know its decimal places**. Nothing more.

**The unsafe way to do what was asked** would have been to pour 152
currencies into `CURRENCIES`. That would have:

- told all 16 money-path consumers those currencies are collectable;
- required 150 invented donation floors, ceilings and caps — the
  unexercisable path `docs/81` forbids;
- let a campaign exist in a currency no gateway can settle, and because
  `(campaign_id, currency)` makes a campaign's currency **immutable once any
  donation exists** (`docs/92`), stranded it there permanently.

So adding a country is a data change in the generated tables. Making its
currency collectable is a separate, deliberate edit to `CURRENCIES` when a
gateway exists. **[VERIFIED]** `getCurrency('NGN')` still throws; a test
asserts it, and `checkDonationAmount` still answers *"This campaign is in a
currency we cannot take payments in yet."*

Campaign currency is also **not settable from any request** — no route or
validator accepts it, and the column defaults to USD. **[VERIFIED by search]**

## 3. Sources, and the one that was rejected

Everything is generated by `scripts/generate-world-currencies.py` into
committed files, so there is no network at build or run time. Each generated
file carries its sources and their publication dates.

| Source | Used for | Version / date |
|---|---|---|
| Unicode CLDR `currencyData.json` (`cldr-core@46`) | country → currency, display digits | CLDR 46, Unicode 16.0.0 |
| ISO 4217 register (SIX Group) | legal minor units | published **2026-09-17** |
| IANA `zone.tab` + `backward` | timezone → country | canonical tz repo |
| This runtime's ICU | independent exponent cross-check | Node 22 |

**The ISO register was rejected for the country mapping**, and the measurement
is why. It keys its entries by country **name**, not code, and joining those
names against ICU's region names scored **196 of 280**, with **84 misses
including the United Arab Emirates, the United Kingdom, the Netherlands,
Russia, South Korea, Hong Kong and the Philippines**. A name join would have
assigned the wrong currency to major countries silently. CLDR keys on ISO 3166
alpha-2 and carries `_from` / `_to` / `_tender`, so current legal tender is
*selected* rather than guessed. **[VERIFIED]**

## 4. ⚠ ISO and CLDR disagree about 13 currencies, and both are right

**[VERIFIED 2026-10-24]**

| | ISO `CcyMnrUnts` | CLDR digits |
|---|---|---|
| IQD (Iraqi dinar) | 3 | 0 |
| AFN, ALL, IRR, KPW, LAK, LBP, MGA, MMK, RSD, SOS, SYP, YER | 2 | 0 |

They answer different questions. ISO defines the subunit — the fils, the
qindarkë, the Somali cent. CLDR records that nobody quotes it. Displaying IQD
with three decimals would print fils that no Iraqi price uses.

This table is for **display**, so CLDR governs `WORLD_CURRENCY_EXPONENTS`, and
the ISO figure is emitted beside it as `ISO_MINOR_UNITS` with the
disagreements listed explicitly in `EXPONENT_DIVERGENCES`.

**The rule that protects money:** none of the 13 may become a collectable
currency until someone has checked which convention the gateway expects.
A test enforces it (`NONE of them is collectable`). For IQD the two readings
differ by a factor of **1,000**, so this is the 100×-class bug waiting for
whoever wires that rail.

Every one of the 152 exponents is cross-checked against this runtime's own ICU
on every build — two independent sources, and the check keeps running rather
than happening once at generation time.

## 5. ⛔ `CF-IPCountry` does not exist here, and `docs/26` is wrong about the edge

**[VERIFIED 2026-10-24]** `docs/26` describes a Cloudflare edge in front of
Fly, with "Layer 1 — Cloudflare edge (HTML + assets)" caching. It is not in
the request path:

- every DNS record for `lastdonor.org` reports **`proxied: false`**;
- the apex `A` record points **straight at the Fly IP** (66.241.124.43);
- a response from `lastdonor.org` carries **no `cf-ray`**, and its `server`
  header is `Fly/…` — identical to hitting the origin directly.

So Cloudflare adds no country header, no caching and no WAF for web traffic.
The cron Worker of `docs/66` is real and unaffected; it calls the app, it does
not front it. `docs/26` has been amended to point here. Fly adds no country
header either — only `fly-request-id`.

An IP-geolocation service was rejected: a paid external dependency on every
render, and it turns a cosmetic figure into a privacy question.

## 6. ⚠ The guess this refuses to make

```
new Intl.Locale('en').maximize().region  === 'US'
new Intl.Locale('sw').maximize().region  === 'TZ'
```

**[VERIFIED on Node 22]** Those are CLDR's statistically likeliest regions for
a language, not facts about the person holding the device. `maximize()` would
confidently tell a Kenyan on an English-only browser that they are in the
United States and show them dollars, with nothing indicating it had guessed —
and would place a Swahili speaker in Tanzania rather than Kenya.

So `countryFromLanguageTags` reads a region **only where the tag states one**
(`en-KE`), and otherwise reports nothing and the UI renders nothing. A test
proves the trap is real by asserting both the `maximize()` output and our
refusal side by side. A mutation that reinstates `maximize()` kills 4 tests.

**Timezone takes precedence** over the language region, because it reflects
where the device says it is: a Kenyan browsing in `en-US` Chrome is the common
case, and `Africa/Nairobi` is right where `en-US` is wrong.

## 7. ⚠ `zone.tab`, not `zone1970.tab` — and the error the generator caught

IANA marks `zone.tab` obsolescent and points at `zone1970.tab`. For *this*
question `zone1970.tab` is the wrong file: it deliberately **merges countries
whose clocks have agreed since 1970**, so Uganda and Kenya share
`Africa/Nairobi` there and a visitor in Kampala resolves to **Kenya** — shown
shillings of the wrong country. `zone.tab` keeps one zone per country
(`UG Africa/Kampala`) and has **418 entries, exactly the number ICU knows**,
where `zone1970.tab` has 312.

The first version of the generator filled the alias gap by inheriting each
`backward` link's **target** country. Its own sanity gate refused to emit:

```
REFUSING TO EMIT:
  - Africa/Kampala -> KE (expected UG)
```

A link means *"the same clock"*, not *"the same country"*. `Africa/Kampala`
links to `Africa/Nairobi`. So `zone.tab` is applied first and the links only
fill genuine **renames** — `Asia/Calcutta` → `Asia/Kolkata`, `Europe/Kiev` →
`Europe/Kyiv` — where the country is identical by construction.

Links are needed because **ICU does not canonicalize**: measured,
`Intl.DateTimeFormat('en',{timeZone:'Africa/Kampala'}).resolvedOptions().timeZone`
returns `Africa/Kampala` unchanged, and a phone in Uganda reports exactly
that. Coverage is now **418 of 418** ICU zones, asserted by a test that will
fail when Node ships a new ICU with zones the committed table lacks.

## 8. Exchange rates: collectable required, known best-effort

The FX snapshot previously required a rate for every supported currency and
rejected the whole table otherwise. With 152 currencies that would have been
fragile: the primary publishes 166 codes and the fallback 288, neither a
superset of our 152, and one absent currency would have stopped **every**
conversion on the site.

Now collectable currencies are **required** (a table without KES cannot price
the one rail that takes money) and the rest are **best effort** — a missing
rate just means `convertMinor` returns null for that currency and the UI shows
nothing, the same refusal it already makes for a stale snapshot.

## 9. What the visitor sees

One client component, `LocalAmount`, which must be client-side because the
only country signals are `Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().timeZone`
and `navigator.languages`.

It renders the **server's** figure first — a USD equivalent for a non-USD
campaign — and swaps to the detected currency after mount, so there is no
hydration mismatch and no flash of nothing. Converting a currency into itself
tells the reader nothing, so a visitor in Kenya reading a KES appeal sees no
approximation at all.

Everything still goes through `ApproxMoney`, so every converted figure carries
the word "about", the date of the rate and the provider attribution that
`docs/102` established as a licence requirement.

## 10. One superseded test, replaced rather than deleted

`money.test.ts` asserted that `formatMoney(100, 'NGN')` **throws** — "refuses
to format in a currency with no rail behind it". That rule was right while the
only currencies named were collectable ones, and the owner's instruction makes
"renderable but not collectable" the intended state for 150 of them.

The guarantee moved rather than vanished. Formatting widened to any known
currency; the **money** path did not. `minorToMajor` and `majorToMinor` still
throw for NGN, and `formatMoney` now does its own division specifically so
that `minorToMajor` can stay strict — it is called by
`payhero-settlement.ts:189` to check what a real payment settled for.
`formatMoney` also still throws for `ZZZ`, `BTC`, `XAU` and `DOGE`.

## 12. Not checked / still open

- **Nothing is deployed.** Production runs v95.
- **No gateway was added**, which is what the owner asked for. 150 of the 152
  currencies are display-only, and the 13 ISO/CLDR divergences are blocked
  from becoming collectable by a test.
- **The detection is not verified against a real foreign browser.** It is
  tested against stubbed timezone and language values and against every zone
  ICU knows; nobody has loaded the page from Kampala. **[UNKNOWN]**
- **A VPN or a traveller gets their current location, not their home
  country.** That is the intended reading of "where is this visitor", but it
  means a Kenyan in London sees pounds.
- **`Accept-Language` parsing exists and is unused.** `parseAcceptLanguage`
  and `countryFromLanguageTags` work server-side, but nothing calls them
  there, because the server has no timezone and the language region alone was
  not judged worth a server render. It is there for whoever wants a
  server-rendered first paint.
- **Multi-currency zones are approximated.** `Africa/Abidjan` covers about a
  dozen countries and resolves to its primary, Côte d'Ivoire. A timezone
  narrows the country; it does not always determine it.
- **The generated tables will drift.** CLDR, ISO and tzdata all move. Re-run
  `python3 -I scripts/generate-world-currencies.py`; the ICU and timezone
  coverage tests are what will tell you it is due.
